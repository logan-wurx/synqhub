# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Gamers and gear enthusiasts with a desk full of peripherals from different makers: mice, keyboards, controllers,
headphones and IEMs, headsets, monitors, webcams and lights. They want each one tuned without installing every maker's
app. They come to SYNQ in short sessions, often between games: change DPI before a match, check a battery, set a lamp's
colour, run a checkup when something feels off. Same screens in the desktop app (Windows, Linux) and on the website
(synqhub.app, Chrome or Edge).

## Product Purpose

One light app for every device. Settings are written to the device itself, so they stay without SYNQ running. Careful
by default: a backup before the first change, every change read back, never firmware. Nothing runs in the background
unless SYNQ Helper is turned on. Success looks like this: someone opens SYNQ, sees all their gear on one page, changes
what they came to change in seconds, and trusts it more than the makers' own apps.

## Brand Personality

Precise and premium like well-made hardware, warm and personal like a setup you built yourself. Calm confidence, never
hype. The page says "Your setup.", not "Device manager". Voice: plain, short and specific. Device names and real numbers,
no marketing words, no exclamation marks.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI glow: blurry colour orbs, purple gradients, everything glowing. The user rejected exactly this.
- The settings spreadsheet: endless rows of toggles and dropdowns with no hierarchy.
- A generic sidebar settings app, and violet accents.
- A paper spec sheet, or a staged desk scene (rejected directions).

## Design Principles

1. **Familiar structure, surprising details.** Keep things where people expect them: the logo top left, sections in the
   dock, the way back at the top left of a device page. Spend the creativity inside that structure, on small, pleasant
   moments (most advanced yet acceptable), never on moving the furniture.
2. **Show the real thing.** Real product photos and the numbers the device itself reported. Never a stand-in, never a
   number SYNQ made up.
3. **Chunk to three or four.** Each screen leads with the one or two things people come to change, and groups the rest
   into a few named sections. Comparisons show the difference, not two full lists.
4. **Safety first, excitement second.** The first feeling is "this won't break my gear": visible backups, Allow changes,
   read-back, calm feedback, nothing jarring. Delight comes after trust.
5. **Hand the rational mind its reasons.** Say what a change does, that it's saved on the device and that it can be
   undone, in plain words, right where the change happens.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and full keyboard use (the app's own accessibility and keyboard sweeps keep this). Reduced motion
is honoured: no looping or sliding motion when the system asks for less. Colour is never the only signal: every status
dot has words beside it.
